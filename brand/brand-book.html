01 · Story

A bridge for people who need French to get somewhere.

La langue est le pont. L’occasion est de l’autre côté.

Who we are, in the client’s words

FrancoBridge Consulting is a language education and professional development firm that helps individuals gain the French language confidence and skills needed to succeed academically, professionally, and through immigration pathways. We empower clients to thrive and integrate fully within Canada’s bilingual and Francophone communities across North America.

Three voice rules

  • Say the level, the hours and the price. A1 to C1, 20, 40 or 60 hours, one hour for $100. Plain numbers, every time, and what is not included is said just as plainly: exam fees, regulated immigration advice.
  • Warm, not loud. Encouraging and steady, the way a good teacher is. No exclamation marks, no hype, and never a promise about a visa, a score or a job. We promise the preparation, not the outcome.
  • English at the door, French inside. The site reads in English because the reader is still learning. Every headline that matters carries a French line under it, and the French is always correct: accents on capitals, proper apostrophes, no quotation marks around a tagline.

The services, as the pamphlet names them

Language Coaching · Government of Canada Second Language Evaluation Preparation · TCF/TEF and DELF/DALF Exam Preparation · French Immigration Pathway Support · Translation Services · Academic Support · Interview Preparation for Bilingual Roles. The site adds Professional French from the original brief, and keeps TCF/TEF first.

02 · The mark

An F and a B, drawn in one line.

The monogram is four open strokes of one weight, with round caps and round joins: an F whose top bar hairpins back into a second stem, and a B whose two bowls are arcs of one circle. Beside it, the wordmark: “FrancoBridge” in red, “Consulting Inc.” under it in blue, both in Avenir. The mark is the bridge; the name says what crosses it.

The lockup, on white. The one signature: the mark at the left, the two lines at the right. The site header and everything on light.

The lockup, on blue. Everything white, as on the business card and the pamphlet’s back. The footer, social, the cover of anything.

One colour. For a stamp, an embroidery, a fax. The name takes the ink like everything else.

The mark alone. Avatars, the app icon, and any space too small for the name.

Construction

Units: the mark is 306 wide and 520 tall; every stroke is 26 wide. The stems stand at x = 13, 67.75 and 120; the bars lie at y = 13, 61, 179, 231, 343 and 507. The top bar hairpins on a radius of 24; the outer corner turns on 27.5, the second on 15.5, the two inner corners on 6. The bowls of the B are arcs of a circle of radius 92.25 centred on x = 159.75, so they meet the bars at an angle rather than tangent, as in the original.

The lockup is 1116 wide and 353 tall. “FrancoBridge” is drawn 1.38 times its size in the client’s artwork, and “Consulting Inc.” at 0.80 of that, so the two lines run to about the same width while the name leads by weight and colour; the second line sits close under the first, with an extra 0.05 of a cap height between them. The mark stands 1.31 times the height of the text block, close to the client’s artwork, and the two lines are centred on it, cap top of “FrancoBridge” to baseline of “Consulting Inc.”. The text starts 243 from the left, two of the mark’s stroke widths clear of it. “FrancoBridge” and “Consulting” are the client’s own lettering, traced; “Inc.” is set in Avenir Light to match, on the same baseline and x-height. The name never moves under the mark; there is no stacked lockup.

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space is two stroke widths, 52 units, on every side: a little more than the gap between the mark and the name. Nothing else, not even the tagline, comes inside it.

52 52

Minimum size. The lockup, 101 px wide (32 px tall), as in the site’s header on a phone. Below that the descriptor closes up, so the mark goes alone, down to 24 px tall. If a space can’t hold 24 px, it doesn’t get the mark.

03 · Signatures

Four things nobody else has.

Four small habits that make a page ours. Two come from the mark and the client’s print, two from the voice. Together they are how you tell a FrancoBridge page from any other language firm at a glance. Use them on everything with the name on it.

01

The wave

The client’s print sweeps a blue field across the page in one S-curve with a gold sliver along its crest, and the monogram is one line with round ends. On a page the wave is the one large shape we draw, running continuously from card to card along the foot of the service cards. It is the pamphlet’s own curve, drawn as two copies of it, gold behind and blue in front, with the small corner where its two halves meet smoothed out. Buttons, photographs and blocks stay square-cornered, so the curve stays special.

02

Red is the name

Red is “FrancoBridge” in the wordmark, the frame of one service card in three, and the rule beside a note that matters (exam fees not included). Never a surface, never running text, never a button. Gold is the accent: a rule beside the mission, the point in the marquee, the hover on a button. Buttons are blue.

03

Two lines

Prepare with purpose.
Préparez-vous avec méthode.

Every headline that matters carries its French under it, quiet and a weight lighter. English is the door; French is the room. The French is set properly: accents on capitals, proper apostrophes, no quotation marks around a tagline, and never a machine translation left unchecked.

04

Plain numbers

LevelA1 to C1
FormatOnline · 20, 40 or 60 h
First step1 hour · $100 CAD

Every offer names its level, its format and its price in a small table before anyone has to ask. Exam fees not included, say so. Regulated advice referred out, say so. It is the most trustworthy thing a firm can do.

04 · Don’t

Six ways the mark gets broken.

Stretch or squash it.

Recolour it. The name is red, the rest blue; on blue, everything white; and nothing else.

Put it on a busy image or a pattern, or across the wave. Use a blue panel.

Rotate it. The bars are level.

Box it, outline it, or add a shadow.

Set it on red. Red is the name, never a ground.

05 · Colour

Blue, white, gold, red. Nothing else.

Four colours, all from the client’s own artwork. The ground is white. Type, buttons, the mark and the footer are the logo’s blue. The logo’s red is the name, and a mark on the page. Gold, from the curves on the pamphlet and card, is the accent. There is no black, no grey and no second blue: where a page needs a lighter shade, for a hairline, a quiet block or secondary type, it takes the blue at reduced strength, the blue mixed with white. The one gradient runs from blue to white.

05 · Type

Avenir Next in print. Figtree on the web.

The wordmark and the client’s print are Avenir Next: Heavy for headings, Demi Bold for the name, Regular for text. Avenir Next is a licensed font and not available on the web for free, so the site sets everything in Figtree, the closest open face: the same geometric round forms, single-storey g, open counters. Headings take Figtree Semibold, set close; text its Regular. The wordmark on the site is Avenir Next still, traced from the artwork into outlines, so the logo never depends on a font.

06 · In use

An editorial school layout, in the client’s colours.

The site follows a light, editorial school layout: a fixed white header with a hairline, a 1280 px centred column with 64 px gutters, 80 px between sections, type set semibold and large, and cards and banners closed with the pamphlet’s wave. The client’s print is bolder: blue fields with a sweep of gold, white type, the lockup in white. Both are the same identity; the page is the quiet version.

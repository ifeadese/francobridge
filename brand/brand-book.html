01 · Story

A bridge, built in Ottawa, for people who need French to get somewhere.

« La langue est le pont. L’occasion est de l’autre côté. »

The official story, from the brief. FrancoBridge Consulting Inc. is a French language education, professional development and pathway guidance centre based in Ottawa, Ontario. We help aspiring immigrants, newcomers, students and professionals develop the French language skills, confidence and career readiness needed to access educational, professional and Francophone opportunities in Canada and beyond.

Our programmes combine structured French language instruction with practical communication, professional development and specialised preparation for French-language proficiency examinations. Our approach emphasises practical communication, measurable progress and personalised support.

FrancoBridge is particularly focused on helping learners use French as a tool for immigration, education, employment and career advancement.

Three voice rules

  • Say the level, the hours and the price. A1 to C1, 20, 40 or 60 hours, one hour for $100. Plain numbers, every time, and what is not included is said just as plainly: exam fees, regulated immigration advice.
  • Warm, not loud. Encouraging and steady, the way a good teacher is. No exclamation marks, no hype, and never a promise about a visa, a score or a job. We promise the preparation, not the outcome.
  • English at the door, French inside. The site reads in English because the reader is still learning. Every headline carries a French line under it, and the French is always correct: guillemets, narrow no-break spaces, accents on capitals.

02 · The mark

An arch bridge, and the Peace Tower at its heart.

One circular arch springs from the deck and passes behind the tower; hangers fall from it on an even rhythm, and four capped piers stand on the second and fourth. At the centre, the Peace Tower, with the maple leaf of the national flag in red. The bridge is the language; the tower is Ottawa and Canada on the other side. Under it, the name and the descriptor in Marcellus, the face of the original, both outlined, so the mark never depends on a font.

Stacked, on blue. The one lockup: the name always sits under the bridge. Footers, social, the cover of anything.

Stacked, on ivory. The site header and everything on light. The bridge, the tower and the name in navy; the leaf stays red.

One colour. For a stamp, an embroidery, a fax. The maple leaf becomes a cutout in the tower.

The mark alone. Avatars and any space too small for the name. The favicon crops to the tower and the crown of the arch.

Construction

Units: the deck is 860 long and 24 thick. The arch is a circular segment springing 405 either side of the centre line and rising 185 above the deck, 24 thick; it stops 11 units short of the spire’s flanks, cut parallel to them. Hangers, 8 wide, fall every 66 from the centre line; the piers, 50 wide with a 66-wide cap and a 5-unit reveal, stand on the second and fourth. The tower is 100 wide, its cornice 112, its spire 90 at the base and 300 from deck to tip.

The name spans the deck, 40 under it, at 0.09 em tracking; the descriptor is 0.74 of its width at 0.2 em, between two rules. Marcellus has one weight, so both lines are weighted up with a stroke in their own colour: 4% of the cap height for the name, 7% for the descriptor. The name never moves beside the bridge; there is no horizontal lockup.

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space is the height of the piers and their caps, 50 units, on every side. Nothing else, not even the tagline, comes inside it.

Minimum size. Stacked, 90 px wide (56 px tall). Below that the descriptor closes up, so the mark goes alone, down to 48 px wide. If a space can’t hold 48 px, it doesn’t get the mark.

03 · Signatures

Four things nobody else has.

Four small habits that make a page ours. Two come from the mark, two from the voice. Together they are how you tell a FrancoBridge page from any other language school at a glance. Use them on everything with the name on it.

01

The arch

B1

The semicircle from the mark is the one shape we draw with. The pattern strips under the banners are built from arches, quarter-circles and the sun; the ringed arrow on a tertiary link is the arch closed. Everything else, buttons, photographs and blocks, is square-cornered, so the arch stays special.

02

The sun

The sun under the arch is the opportunity. On a page the red is a mark, never a surface and never text: it sits in the maple leaf of the logo, in the pattern strips under every banner, and as the rule beside a note that matters (exam fees not included). Buttons are black. Nothing else is red.

03

Two lines

Prepare with purpose.
« Préparez-vous avec méthode. »

Every headline that matters carries its French under it, in Crimson Pro italic, quiet; on the about page and on each service page the French line becomes a whole pastel band. English is the door; French is the room. The French is set properly: guillemets with narrow no-break spaces, accents on capitals, and never a machine translation left unchecked.

Every offer names its level, its format and its price in a small table before anyone has to ask. Exam fees not included, say so. Regulated advice referred out, say so. It is the most trustworthy thing a school can do.

04 · Don’t

Six ways the mark gets broken.

Stretch or squash it.

Recolour it. The leaf is red, the bridge takes navy or ivory, and nothing else.

Put it on a busy image or a gradient. Use a blue panel.

Rotate it. The deck is level; it is a bridge.

Box it, outline it, or add a shadow.

Set it on red. Red is the leaf, never a ground.

05 · Colour

Warm white, near-black, and the brand in tints.

The ground is a warm white, never pure. Type and buttons are black, a warm near-black. A light grey makes the blocks, the footer and the quiet bands; a hairline grey draws the rules. The brand blue and red stay in the mark, the patterns and small accents, and each lends a pastel tint for the banners, with a yellow borrowed for the card on the hero photo.

05 · Type

Crimson Pro speaks. Figtree explains.

Two families. Crimson Pro, light, set at 100% line height with tight tracking, for everything that carries the voice: headlines, the French line, large numbers. It is never bold. Figtree, plain and friendly, for everything people read at length, and for labels and buttons. The name in the mark is outlined Marcellus; nothing else on the site uses it.

06 · In use

An editorial school layout.

The site follows a light, editorial school layout: a fixed white header with a hairline, a 1280 px centred column with 64 px gutters, 80 px between sections, type set light and large, and pastel banners closed with a strip of pattern. Documents follow the same rules on paper.
